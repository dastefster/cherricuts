CherriCuts Privacy Policy

Effective October 16, 2026

CherriCuts doesn't collect your personal data. Your code stays on your device, with one exception: when you tap Export, your shortcut is sent to a signing service so iOS will accept it.

What stays on your device

  • The Cherri code you write in the editor.
  • Your settings: editor font, font size, whether Export asks first, and whether you've seen the welcome screen.

All of this is stored locally in the app's own storage. Compiling happens entirely on your device and uses no network connection.

What leaves your device: Export

iOS only imports signed shortcuts. When you tap Export, CherriCuts compiles your code and sends the compiled, unsigned shortcut to HubSign (hubsign.routinehub.services), a signing service run by RoutineHub. HubSign returns the signed shortcut, and CherriCuts hands it to you through the iOS share sheet.

The request contains the compiled shortcut (which includes anything you wrote into it, such as text and URLs), the name "Shortcut", and the CherriCuts and Cherri compiler version numbers. As with any internet request, HubSign's servers can also see your device's IP address.

CherriCuts doesn't control HubSign, and RoutineHub's own policies govern how it handles requests. Please don't put passwords or other secrets in code you export. By default CherriCuts asks you before exporting, and you can change that in Settings.

What we don't do

  • No accounts or sign-in.
  • No ads.
  • No analytics or tracking tools.
  • No selling or sharing of your data.
  • The developer receives no data from the app itself.

Links

Some screens link to websites, such as the Cherri language reference. Those sites have their own privacy practices.

TestFlight and the App Store

If you install CherriCuts through TestFlight or the App Store, Apple may collect crash reports and usage information under its own privacy policy, and the developer may see some of it through App Store Connect. If you submit feedback through TestFlight, such as a screenshot or comment, the developer receives it.

Shortcuts you create

Shortcuts you export belong to you. Once they're in the Shortcuts app, Apple's terms and privacy policy apply to them, as does the privacy policy of any third-party app the shortcut uses.

Children

CherriCuts isn't directed at children, and it doesn't collect personal information from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with an updated date.
